This is a big enough canvas to discuss liquids from the scale of oceans down to droplets in the clouds, while also examining the curious liquid crystals in the onboard entertainment system, the beverages served by the airline stewards, and of course the aviation fuel that keeps a plane in the stratosphere. - As useful as liquid helium is, it’s also quite unruly. It will operate successfully to cool MRI machines to –269ºC, but cool it down a few more degrees to –272ºC, and it enters what we call a superfluid state. In this state, all the atoms in the liquid occupy a single quantum state, which is to say that all of the billions and billions of helium molecules act as if they are a single molecule, giving the liquid odd powers – it has no viscosity for instance, and will spontaneously flow up out of a container. It will even be able to flow through solid materials, finding its way through the object’s atomic-sized defects without any resistance.

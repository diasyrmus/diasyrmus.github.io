Between Rotterdam and Antwerp, I see a church across several fields in the beautifully clear sunlight we’ve been having in north-western europe this april. Chilly in the morning, warm in the afternoon, the clearest possible sunlight. 

It seemed ideal in some way. Redolent of the possibilities of fiction. 

And yet, I found myself incapable of opening myself to it, which is also opening oneself to poetry, to possibility, due to the pre-occupations of work, and my own anxiety, of doing ‘the wrong thing’ by spending a bit more on expenses (a constant battle - what is the rhyme or trick to not worry about that?), of losing my job (in the sense that this would be the end of everything, rather than the start of something else - what’s that dog on the raft ‘different’ line?), of the fact that I am getting rid of people at work, and closing operations, based on my own decisions. Grotesque. 

Someone has to do it, and maybe it is done better by a sympathetic person than allowing only the very worst, who enjoy this sort of thing to do it, but I’m not sure I believe that. I think if you participate in this sort of thing, you participate in the same quality that you fear and despise in others.

What was that line I just read in this article?

https://www.liberalcurrents.com/remembering-the-weight-of-life-and-death/

But the greatest sin is that this anxiety blocks me out from feeling, from perceiving the world, because after all if everything else goes wrong, we still have the world around us, in its fractal glory.

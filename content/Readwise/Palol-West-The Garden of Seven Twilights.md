The quintessential ones are those stripped of all vegetation, which contain only sixteen rocks in the sand, symbols of the sixteen disciples of Buddha who spread his doctrine all over the world; for example the Ryoan-ji garden or the Shuon-an in Kyoto. In some, the rocks are so arranged that a portion of them always block the view of others, no matter where you stand; because of this, you can never see them all at once.
